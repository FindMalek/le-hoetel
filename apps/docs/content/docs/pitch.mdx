## The one idea

Your guests leave and you never hear from them again, and you pay a commission every time they come back. We fix both.

The guest scans a QR code, leaves a real contact and a clear consent, and the hotel can then thank, recognise and invite them back on WhatsApp and email. The hotel owns the list.

## Opening line

> "When a guest checks out, do you have a way to reach them again that you own? Not the booking site, not the tour operator, yours?"

Then stop talking. The answer is almost always "no" or "some guests, in a notebook". That is the conversation.

Alternative opener for a hotel that already worries about reviews:

> "What happens today when a guest leaves unhappy? How do you find out, and when?"

## Discovery questions

Ask these before showing anything. Write the answers down.

1. How do most of your guests book: direct, a booking site, a tour operator?
2. What share of your guests are Tunisian, and which markets are the rest?
3. Do you have contacts for your past guests? Where are they kept?
4. How do you hear about a problem during a stay? After the stay?
5. How many Google reviews do you get a month, and who answers them?
6. When is low season, and what do you do today to fill it?
7. Do you have a spa or a restaurant that is not full?
8. How much commission did you pay last year to booking sites or tour operators? Roughly.
9. Who would use a tool like this day to day: reception, a manager, you?
10. Do guests use WhatsApp with your staff already?
11. Have you tried a loyalty card or a newsletter before? What happened?
12. If this worked, what would you want to see after 60 days?

## The three pains

Find out which one hurts most and lead with it.

| Pain | Lead with | What they see first |
|---|---|---|
| Reviews and reputation | Unhappy guest alert and the Google review invite | A low score alert to the manager within a day of checkout, more reviews in month 1 |
| Empty low season | Offers to their own list | A birthday or low season offer with a redemption QR, so results are measured |
| Tour operator dependence | Capture, so they finally own contacts | A growing list of consented guests with the hotel's own branding |

The order a hotel sees value, from fastest to slowest:

1. Week 1: unhappy guest alerts, fixed the same day
2. Month 1: more Google reviews
3. Months 2 to 3: a contact list the hotel owns, offers for low season, the spa and the restaurant
4. Month 6 plus: direct rebooking saves commission

## Objection handling

### "It is too expensive" or "How much?"

- Do not open with a price. Open with the pilot: free for 60 to 90 days, you pay only if you see results.
- Ask what they pay today in commission. One direct rebooking can pay for months of the tool.
- Our plans are a hypothesis, so ask: "What would this be worth to you per month if it worked?" and record the answer in the price-test sheet below.
- Offer annual prepay for two months free only after the pilot proves it.

### "Our guests do not use WhatsApp" or "WhatsApp is spam"

- In Tunisia WhatsApp is where people already talk, so it is the channel guests answer.
- Every guest ticks a separate yes for WhatsApp and a separate yes for email. No yes, no message.
- Messages are few and relevant: welcome, birthday, one offer at a time. Every message has an unsubscribe or STOP.
- Email works too, and guests choose the channel.

### "Is our guest data safe?" or "Who owns it?"

- The hotel owns the guest data. We only process it, and the contract says so.
- Consent is logged per channel with date, source and wording version. Export and delete are available at any time.
- We never sell or reuse a guest list. We never collect passport numbers, payment cards or phone location.
- Be honest about what is open: the consent wording and hosting get a legal check before the first pilot. We are not lawyers. See [data and consent rules](/docs/data-rules).

### "My staff are too busy"

- The guest does the typing, not the staff: they scan the QR and fill the form on their own phone.
- Messages and surveys run by themselves. Staff only see an alert when a guest is unhappy.
- Staff can add a guest or upload a CSV if they have an old list, so nobody types twice.
- We set up the QR kit and train reception. Ryan is the first line of support.

### "We pay commission to the tour operator, what can you do?"

- Honest answer: the biggest gain is also the slowest, six months or more.
- Start with the contact: with a list you own, a returning guest can book you directly next time.
- Tour operator packages mean you may never see the guest contact today. The QR on site is how the hotel gets it.
- We do not promise a number. We measure rebookings and offer redemptions together during the pilot.

### Other quick ones

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "We have a PMS" | You do not need to connect it to start. CSV import now, a connector later. |
| "We tried this before" | What happened? Usually no perk for the guest or nobody read the feedback. We fix both. |
| "Not now, it is high season" | Capture needs only a QR in the rooms. The survey and the review invite run on their own. |
| "Send me an email" | Offer a 15 minute setup visit instead and ask for a date. |

## Opening the demo hotel

The demo hotel is Sunset Bay Resort on staging. It never sends a message, and production has no demo data.

1. Before the meeting, open the staging dashboard on your phone and sign in as `sales@demo.ritorno.test`. Ask Malek for its password. It opens nothing but this view only account, so the phone may remember it. Never save another demo password on a phone you hand over
2. Switch the language to the owner's (French, Arabic or English)
3. Walk through guests, offers, campaigns, feedback and QR codes. The account is view only, so you can hand the phone over

The steps below that create something (the guest scan, a new offer) need an account that can write, such as `owner.sunset-bay@demo.ritorno.test`. Do not type a real person's details into the demo: anyone with a demo login can read them.

## Five minute demo script

The demo is on a phone and on a laptop with the demo hotel. Everything uses fake data.

1. **0:00, the problem (30 seconds).** Restate the pain the owner named. No slides.
2. **0:30, the guest scan (60 seconds).** Hand them a phone. Scan the QR, show the hotel's logo and colours, pick the language (French, Arabic, English), fill name and phone, tick WhatsApp and email separately, see the thank you and the perk.
3. **1:30, the hotel sees the guest (45 seconds).** Open the dashboard. The guest is there with a consent record. Show the guest count and the consented channels.
4. **2:15, a message (45 seconds).** Show the welcome and the birthday message as the guest sees them, with the hotel's branding. Point at the unsubscribe or STOP line.
5. **3:00, the unhappy guest (60 seconds).** Show a low survey score and the alert the manager gets. "You find out in a day, not on Google in a month." Then show the Google review invite sent to everyone.
6. **4:00, an offer (45 seconds).** Create a low season offer, show the redemption QR, show how redemptions are counted.
7. **4:45, the ask (15 seconds).** "Free for 60 to 90 days. Can we put QR codes in 10 rooms and the restaurant next week?"

<Callout type="info" title="Status">
The demo describes the first release. Show only what is live. If a step is not built yet, show a mock and say so.
</Callout>

## Pilot agreement outline

An outline to discuss with the hotel. It is not a legal document: a lawyer reviews the final text.

1. **Parties and contact.** Hotel, the legal entity that provides the service, a named contact on each side. The invoicing entity is still an [open decision](/docs/decisions#open-decisions).
2. **Purpose.** A pilot of the guest capture, messaging and survey tool.
3. **Duration.** 60 to 90 days from the day the first QR is live.
4. **What the hotel does.** Put QR codes in rooms and on restaurant tables. Name one person for reception. Send feedback at least every two weeks.
5. **What we do.** Set up the hotel branding, templates and QR kit. Train reception. First line support.
6. **Cost.** Free during the pilot, including setup. Messaging costs on WhatsApp are drawn from a prepaid balance, or free during the pilot if agreed.
7. **Data.** The hotel owns the guest data. We process it for the hotel only. Consent is logged per channel. We never sell or reuse the list. Export and delete on request.
8. **Success measures.** For example: 200 consented guests in month one, survey responses received, at least one offer redeemed. Agree the numbers together.
9. **After the pilot.** The hotel chooses a plan or stops. If it stops, the hotel gets a full export and we delete the data on request.
10. **Ending early.** Either side can stop at any time with notice.
11. **Open points.** Legal review of consent wording and hosting.
